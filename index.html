Sounds and Feelings on the Road

Why does my car pull to one side?

Imagine you are driving on a straight, flat road and you have to keep a light, steady pressure on the steering wheel to stay in your lane. That drift is one of the most common early signs of a problem with Wheel alignment. Alignment controls the angle at which your wheels meet the road. When it drifts out of specification, the car can pull left or right and wear the tires unevenly.

A pull can also come from a difference in tire pressure between the left and right sides. Check your pressures first, since it costs nothing. If the pull continues after your tires are properly inflated, have the alignment checked before the problem spreads to the tread.

What does a vibration at highway speed mean?

Consider a driver who feels a faint shimmy through the steering wheel only between about fifty-five and seventy miles per hour, then smooths out at higher speeds. That pattern often points to a wheel or tire that is out of balance. A small weight imbalance is normal to some degree, but it grows as tread wears or when a weight falls off. Ask whether Tire balancing has been checked, especially after you have had a tire repaired or replaced.

If you feel the vibration mainly through the seat or floor, the rear tires may be the source. Mention where you feel it when you describe the problem to a technician, because that detail helps narrow the cause quickly.

What Your Tread Is Telling You

Why are my tires wearing unevenly?

Picture two tires on the same axle. One has worn smoothly across the center, while the other shows heavy wear on the inside edge. Each pattern tells a different story. Wear on both outer edges usually means the tires have been running underinflated. Wear down the center often means they have been overinflated. Wear on only one edge points toward alignment.

Uneven wear can also come from skipping tire rotation. Front tires on most vehicles wear faster than rear tires, so moving them to different positions at regular intervals helps them wear more evenly and last longer. Your owner’s manual lists the recommended schedule for your vehicle.

How do I know when it is time for New tires?

Use the simple penny test. Place a penny upside down in a tread groove. If you can see the top of Lincoln’s head in several spots, the tread is likely too shallow and it is time to consider New tires. Also look for cracks in the sidewall, bulges, exposed cords, and tires that are much older than their tread suggests. Age matters even when the tread looks good, because rubber can harden and crack over time.

Many states set a legal minimum tread depth of two thirty-seconds of an inch. Shallow tread reduces grip in rain and can make braking distances longer, so it is worth acting before the tread is close to that limit.

Small Punctures and Slow Leaks

Can a slow leak wait until my next trip?

Think about a driver who notices the tire pressure warning light comes on every few mornings. The light clears after driving, then returns after the car sits overnight. That pattern often means a slow leak, usually from a nail or screw in the tread. Waiting can turn a simple repair into a damaged tire, especially if you keep driving on a low tire.

Flat tire repair is often possible when the damage is in the tread area and within repair guidelines, but a technician needs to inspect the tire from the inside to decide. Damage on the sidewall usually calls for replacement. Stopping at the first sign of a slow leak gives you more repair options.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I have my tires checked?

Check tire pressure at least once a month and before long trips. Have the tires inspected during routine service visits, and ask a technician to look at tread wear, balance, and alignment whenever you notice a change in how the car drives.

Is it safe to drive with a warning light on?

If the tire pressure light stays on, check and adjust the pressures as soon as you can. If the tire looks low, drive slowly to a shop rather than continuing on a soft tire. A flashing or persistent warning should be treated as urgent.

Can I just buy the cheapest tire I can find?

Price should not be the only factor. The right tire depends on your vehicle, driving habits, and climate. Ask a technician to explain the tradeoffs between options so you can compare tread life, grip, and noise.
